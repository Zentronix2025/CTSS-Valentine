History of Valentine.

💌 Who was Saint Valentine? Valentine was a Roman priest around 270 AD. Emperor Claudius II outlawed marriage for young men (better soldiers if single). Valentine defied the decree and secretly married couples. Imprisoned and sentenced to death, he allegedly healed the jailer's blind daughter. Before execution he wrote her a letter signed "from your Valentine" — still used today.

🏛️ Ancient roots: Lupercalia Mid‑February held Lupercalia, a Roman fertility festival. Young men drew names of women from a jar – a match for the year. As Christianity spread, the church replaced it with a saint's day. In 496 AD, Pope Gelasius declared February 14 as St. Valentine's Day.

📜 Chaucer & the love birds Medieval poet Geoffrey Chaucer (14th c.) linked Valentine's Day with romance in his poem "Parlement of Foules": "For this was on seynt Volantynys day, Whan euery bryd comyth there to chese his make." This idea spread across Europe, making February 14 the day of lovers and poetic courtship.

💌 First written valentine The oldest known valentine was a poem by Charles, Duke of Orléans (1415) to his wife while imprisoned in the Tower of London. By the 18th century, printed cards replaced hand‑written notes. In the 1840s, Esther Howland (USA) mass‑produced ornate cards – "Mother of the American Valentine". Today over 145 million cards are exchanged yearly.
